# Headcount rollout plan: private beta to public launch

**Owner:** Greg. **Executed by:** the Headcount Marketing office: our own product, run by its AI marketing team (see
[DOGFOOD.md](DOGFOOD.md)). **Status:** ready to start. **Working name:** Headcount. If we rename (NAMING.md shortlists
Officeful), every post below updates in one pass.

## 1. The plan

Over the next eight weeks we take Headcount from a handful of early users to a few hundred businesses in a private,
invite-only beta.

The story we tell is one we can prove every day: **we hired an AI marketing team inside Headcount, and it is launching
Headcount.** Every post, image, video and report in this plan is planned in Headcount meetings, made by Headcount
coworkers, and approved by a human before it goes out. The marketing is the demo.

## 2. The story

**The problem.** Small business owners run everything themselves. They answer customers, chase invoices, post on
social media and plan promotions, usually at 11pm. Chatbots answer questions but don't do the work. Agent builders
need a developer. Hiring is expensive and slow.

**The turn.** What if you could hire a team the way you'd brief a person? You get one Chief of Staff you can talk to,
and coworkers with real faces, voices and crafts:
- a bookkeeper;
- a copywriter;
- a social media manager.

They work in the background and meet in a room when a decision needs several heads. They bring you the outcome:
documents, images, short videos and plans, and nothing leaves the building without your yes.

**The proof.** We didn't write this launch. Our AI marketing team did:
- Samuel, our product marketer, chaired the positioning meeting.
- Leilani writes and schedules our posts.
- Jasmine art-directs our images, and Emre makes our video clips.
- Every Friday, Elif tells us what worked.

We approve their work, and sometimes we argue with them in the meeting room.

**The invitation.** We're letting a few businesses at a time into the office. Bring one job you keep putting off.

### Lines to reuse

- "Hire a team in a sentence. Watch it work."
- "Your Chief of Staff runs the office. You make the calls."
- "Put the team in a room."
- "Nothing leaves the building without your yes."
- "This post was planned in a meeting of AI coworkers. A human approved it."

### Honesty rules (non-negotiable)

1. Every coworker is labelled as AI. We never imply they are people.
2. We use no testimonials, customer counts, logos or metrics we don't have. Beta quotes are used only with written
   permission, and with the person's real name and business.
3. Dogfooding is disclosed: posts made by the AI team say so (for example, "Made by our AI marketing team. Approved by
   us.").
4. Feature claims match the releases page (/releases). If it's not built, it's not in a post.

## 3. Who it's for

| Audience | Who they are | What they want | First job to try |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Online shop owners** (primary) | Shopify and Etsy sellers, one to five people (e.g. a crystal shop) | More sales, social posts, product photos and promotions | "Make a week of Instagram posts for our new amethyst collection" |
| **Local service businesses** | Salons, gyms, cafés, trades | Bookings, follow-ups, reviews and local marketing | "Plan our spring promotion and write the email" |
| **Consultants and small agencies** | One to ten people with clients | Proposals, research and reports in client-ready format | "Research three competitors and give me a deck" |
| **Creators** | Newsletters, podcasts, YouTube | Content calendars, scripts and clips | "Turn this episode into five posts and a 10-second teaser" |

**Beta ideal profile:** the owner is the decision maker, they post on social media at least weekly, they have one
recurring job they hate, and they'll give 15 minutes of feedback.

## 4. Private beta (invite-only)

Sign-up is set to invite-only (`SIGNUP_MODE=invite`). We invite by email, and each beta owner can invite their own team
and start up to three offices.

### Waves

| Wave | When | Offices | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (now) | Week 0 | 10 to 15 | Current users and friends: the people already asking for more |
| 1 | Weeks 1 and 2 | 25 | Online shops (crystal, candles, ceramics, apparel); hand-picked from our network and replies to launch posts |
| 2 | Weeks 3 and 4 | 75 | Add local services and consultants; from the waitlist and referrals |
| 3 | Weeks 5 and 6 | 150 to 300 | Everyone on the waitlist who fits the profile, plus creators |
| Public waitlist | Week 8 | — | Landing CTA becomes "Request an invite"; opens when the success criteria below hold |

### How someone gets in

1. They reply to a post or DM, or fill in the request form (to build: see section 12).
2. Rory, our community manager, screens requests against the profile. A human approves the list.
3. They get the invite email (draft in section 7), sign in with Google, and land in their own office.
4. In week 1 they get a welcome email (day 0), the "try these" email (day 2), the meetings tip (day 5) and a feedback
   ask (day 7).

### What we offer beta users

- Free during the beta, with a generous usage allowance.
- A 20-minute onboarding call, offered to the first 50.
- A weekly 30-minute office-hours call.
- Their name in the releases page "thanks" section, if they want it.

### Success criteria to open the waitlist

| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Activation: hired three or more coworkers and finished one project, or held one meeting, in the first 7 days | ≥ 50% of new offices |
| Week-4 retention: active in week 4 | ≥ 35% |
| Making things: images, videos or Word/PowerPoint exports per active office per week | ≥ 3 |
| Qualitative: "would be very disappointed without it" | ≥ 30% of respondents |
| Trust: no serious incidents (wrong email sent, data seen across offices) | Zero |

## 5. Channels and cadence

All posting goes through Buffer, connected to the marketing office. Buffer reaches X, LinkedIn, Facebook and
Instagram from one queue, and its analytics feed the Friday report.

| Channel | Cadence | Format | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **X** | 1 a day (weekdays) | Short posts, a weekly thread, 10-second clips | Build in public; reply to every beta question |
| **LinkedIn** | 3 a week | Founder voice posts, carousels made from PowerPoint exports, meeting transcripts | The strongest channel for consultants and agencies |
| **Instagram** | 3 Reels and 2 carousels a week, Stories daily | 9:16 clips, before/after images, "meet the team" faces | The strongest channel for shop owners |
| **Facebook** | 3 a week | Reels cross-posted, plus posts in small business groups (by a human, by hand) | Group posts follow each group's rules; never automated |

### Content pillars (rotate weekly)

1. **Meet the team:** one coworker a week, with their face, voice, craft and one job they do. ("This is Leilani. She
   wrote this caption.")
2. **In the room:** 20 to 40 second clips of a real meeting: the chair framing, a disagreement, the summary.
3. **Made by the office:** an image, a video or a Word/PowerPoint document the office made this week, with the
   one-sentence prompt that made it.
4. **Use cases:** a 60-second walkthrough of one job end to end (section 9).
5. **Building in public:** what our AI marketing team did this week, what we approved, and what we rejected and why.
6. **Trust:** approvals, receipts, the audit log and Everyone stop. Short and plain.

## 9. Demos and use cases

### Demo scripts (2 minutes each; recorded once, cut into 20 to 40 second clips)

**A. The crystal shop: from empty office to a week of posts**

1. A new office called Amethyst & Co. The Chief says hello.
2. Type: "We sell healing crystals online on Shopify and Etsy." The Hire page suggests an e-commerce manager, a social
   media manager, a market researcher and a support lead. Hire them.
3. In Talk: "Call a meeting about launching a monthly crystal subscription box." Listen in for 20 seconds, then show
   the summary arriving in Talk.
4. "Make an image of our amethyst gift box on white linen." It appears in the thread.
5. When the meeting ends, the team starts on what it agreed (the chair leads): open the work from the Chief's note
   and download the plan as PowerPoint when it is done.

**B. The bakery: a spring promotion, end to end**

1. "Put together a team to plan our spring promotion with an email and three Instagram posts."
2. Approve the plan in the Inbox and watch the steps and the kickoff meeting.
3. Read the deliverable and download it as Word.
4. The email card: approve it once, and show the receipt in the audit log.

**C. The consultant: research while you keep talking**

1. "Find three competitors for my bookkeeping practice in Austin and compare their prices."
2. "How's it going?" The live step answers. "Also check which ones offer payroll." The steer lands at the next
   checkpoint.
3. Call a meeting of the analyst and the copywriter about positioning. Take the floor once.
4. Download the comparison as Excel.

## 11. Guardrails

- **Approvals:** in weeks 0 to 2 every post is a Buffer **draft** that a human approves in Buffer. From week 3, if the
  drafts needed few edits, the team schedules directly and a human reviews the Friday report. The autonomy dial and
  Everyone stop are the off switches.
- **Platform rules:** we follow the automation and disclosure policies of X, LinkedIn, Meta and Instagram:
  - no automated DMs, no automated replies to strangers, no follow/unfollow;
  - Facebook group posts are always made by a human.
- **Brand safety:** no comparisons that name competitors, no pricing promises during the beta, no health claims
  about crystals (the crystal shop is an example business, not ours).
- **Costs:** each office has daily limits on images, videos and projects. Buffer runs on its free or Essentials plan.
