# Judge scorecard: Owner value and compellingness

> **Lens.** Would a busy owner of a 2–40 person business pay for this, and would they love it? I score five things:
> - **Core loop:** how clear the loop is.
> - **Time back:** time saved and a believable ROI.
> - **Trust:** how trust is built by mechanism.
> - **Useful wow:** wow moments that are useful, not gimmicky.
> - **Name:** the name and positioning.
>
> **Evidence.** The four concept documents (A–D), plus `docs/research/capability-matrix.md` and the three reference overviews. Provenance tags follow the concepts: [BB] biz-bot, [OC] oncloudnine, [PG] personagram-ai, [NEW] white space.

---

## 1. Verdict

| Concept | Core loop (25%) | Time back (25%) | Trust (20%) | Useful wow (15%) | Name (15%) | **Overall** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A: Righthand** | 9 | 9 | 9 | 7 | 6 | **8.5** |
| **D: Headcount** | 8 | 7 | 7 | 9 | 9 | **8.0** |
| **B: Ninth Floor** | 7 | 5 | 7 | 9 | 5 | **6.5** |
| **C: Mainspring** | 6 | 5 | 9 | 5 | 4 | **6.0** |

**Winner under this lens: A (Righthand).** A narrowly beats D.
- **What A does best.** A is the only concept designed around what an owner is actually short of, which is attention. Its loop fits the week the owner already has: Brief, then Deck, then Wrap, then Review. It answers each owner's specific fear with a specific mechanism, and it measures itself on owner outcomes rather than engagement.
- **What D does best.** D has by far the strongest name, category story, pricing and go-to-market.
- **The recommendation.** Build A's product and launch it with D's positioning and packaging.

The weights reflect the question "would they pay for it and keep paying?". Loop and time back decide renewal. Trust decides whether owners delegate anything real. Wow and naming decide whether they try it at all.

## 2. Concept by concept

### A: Righthand (*One chief of staff. A whole staff behind them. Your day back.*): **8.5**

**Strengths**
- **The core loop is explicit and closed** (§4).
  - "Brief → delegations → background work → Deck → Wrap → the next Brief."
  - Every Brief opens with what changed, replayed from the event bus with `?since` [BB].
  - It is the only concept where the loop is a schedule the owner can picture.
- **The design laws are owner laws, not engineering laws** (§1). They are "Decisions, not dashboards", "Trust is earned per action, not granted per agent" and "Faces and voices exist to save time." The third law is the best answer in any concept to the charge that avatars are a gimmick.
- **Three owners, each with a named fear and a named win** (§2).
  - Dana fears off-brand work, Luis fears money moving wrongly, and Priya fears confidentiality breaches and missed deadlines.
  - §11 then claims "Each owner's fear is answered by a mechanism, not a promise", and the document delivers on that claim.
- **The best trust design in the set.**
  - The **autonomy dial per action class** (§7.1) runs Ask → Propose → Act & report → Act quietly and compiles to BB grant kinds.
  - The **track record** (§7.2) is "a résumé built from receipts". It produces promotion proposals, and any reversal causes an automatic demotion (§5.7).
  - Owners already reason about trust this way ("what is being done, and how has it gone?"), and no other concept models it.
- **Time back you can measure.**
  - The **Time-back report** (§10) covers hours saved (calibrated by the owner), dollars spent, dollars per hour saved, and the median time to resolve a decision.
  - The **Decision Deck** clears 5–15 cards in under 5 minutes.
  - **Friday Review replays** play at 1.5× with chapters, "skip" and pause-to-ask (§5.6).
  - "Listening beats reading" (§11) turns voice into time saved rather than time spent.
- **Money you can predict** (§7.3).
  - A forecast range appears before approval, and the budget is reserved before every call.
  - An overrun "raises a hand" with a concrete ask ("Raise by $10?").
  - Voice is metered. The concept also notes that BB's missing `rates` break budgets.
- **Useful wow moments.**
  - Barge-in during the 7:40 Brief while driving (§5.1).
  - The urgency ladder (digest → Deck → push → live) plus a 15-second spoken **handoff card**: who, what, why now, recommendation, options (§5.4).
  - Camera cards routed to two coworkers at once (§5.5).
  - The guest door feeds the raised hand, closing a revenue loop (§5.8 → §5.4).
- **Onboarding promises a first outcome in 60 minutes** (§6).
  - The Righthand proposes three starters built from what it just learned.
  - Connectors start read-only, and write scopes are requested at first need as a Deck card.
  - No model call is made on empty input [PG].
- **Its success measures are owner-value measures** (§13):
  - first outcome in under 60 minutes;
  - at least 60% of decisions resolved from the Brief or Deck;
  - Brief listen-through of at least 70% in week 4;
  - forecasts within ±25%;
  - zero duplicate effects.

**Weaknesses**
- **The name is weak for a category play.** "Righthand" is warm and clear but generic, hard to own in search, and it names the lead agent rather than the product's big promise. "Engagement" is agency and consulting vocabulary that means little to Luis's coffee business.
- **Too many rituals.** The §4 table fires Brief, two Deck sittings and a Wrap every day, plus a Friday Review and a Month-end Board. A busy owner who has just been promised "your day back" gets six fixed touchpoints, which risks becoming a new calendar to babysit.
- **Money can be approved by voice without a safety step** (§5.1). "Approve. Just this one." commits a $6,120 purchase order from a moving car with no read-back or confirmation. B handles this better (§8.5).
- **Avatars sell less of the story.** The MVP Floor is "list-first desk tiles" (§13), and the brief explicitly asks for interactive avatars and listening while they speak. That undersells the demo moment, which D and B make into the hook.
- **Casting may break the track record.** Each engagement casts a new team (Marcus, Juno, Pax, Ines in §5.2). If coworkers are new each time, track records and distilled memory never accumulate. A never states a "staff from the existing team first" rule.
- **Pricing is thin.** It says only "one plan plus usage credits" (§13) and gives no tiers or unit prices.

### D: Headcount (*Hire a team in a sentence. Watch it work. Walk in anytime.*): **8.0**

**Strengths**
- **It has the best name and positioning in the set.**
  - "Headcount" is the owner's own word for staffing, and it says "staff you add without hiring".
  - The category line is memorable and sales-ready: "Chat gave you an answer. Builders gave you a flowchart. Avatars gave you a face. **Headcount gives you staff.**" (§1)
- **It reframes avatars as function.**
  - "Presence is observability for non-engineers… **They are the monitoring UI**" (§1). This is the single most important sentence in the four concepts for defending the avatar investment to a buyer.
  - §11 reinforces it: "every demo leads with the deliverable… The north-star metric is deliverables accepted, not minutes watched."
- **The category map** (§2) contrasts D with chat assistants, agent builders and avatar products. It also names the moat: three hard stacks that usually live in three different companies.
- **The packaging suits owners** (§8).
  - "Humans pay for seats. Coworkers are not seats."
  - One credit pool covers every modality, and "you pay for voice only when someone listens".
  - At the cap, the owner chooses per project whether work stops or raises a hand.
  - Tiers are concrete ($49/$199/$799), with credits pegged to $0.01.
- **The go-to-market is the sharpest** (§9).
  - The wedge is boutique agencies, where billable-hour maths makes the ROI obvious.
  - A keyless demo office runs on Mock [BB] with a seeded showcase coworker [PG].
  - Replays and front doors spread the product.
  - Industry packs include an in-character sceptic ("client CFO", "the auditor").
- **The wow moments are useful, and each is justified with "Why only us"** (§5).
  - "The stand-up in your ears" (§5.5) is the one legitimate use of PG director mode (a script written from real run state, T9), and it lets the owner resolve decisions by interrupting.
  - **Front doors** (§5.6) turn PG's prompt-only `handoff` into a real escalation ("That's Dana's call. I'll get it to her tonight."), followed by a proposed follow-up project. This is the most revenue-adjacent moment in any concept.
- **Headcount maths** (§7.5) attaches a human-equivalent estimate to each plan step: "about 31 hours of work this week for 2,140 credits." That is a renewal argument.
- **The magic is measured** (§10). A sentence becomes a staffed pod at p50 under 45 s. The Chief of Staff's first audio arrives at p50 under 1.5 s. At least half of approved plans reach an accepted deliverable. Zero external effects happen without approval.

**Weaknesses**
- **The daily loop is thinner than A's.** D is a list of seven moments, not a rhythm. There is no Deck batching, no urgency ladder and no evening wrap, and the only recurring touchpoint is the stand-up.
- **Trust does not grow.** The "grant ladder" is a metric (§7.1), not a mechanism. There is no per-action-class dial, no track record and no automatic demotion.
- **Some success metrics reward spending time, not saving it.** "≥60% walk into a room in week 1, ≥30% take the floor" (§10) measures engagement. For a busy owner, minutes spent listening are a cost unless they replace reading.
- **White-labelling works against the wedge.** "Made with Headcount" goes on replays, front doors and deliverable portals (§7.3, §9.3), yet white-label is only on the $799 tier (§8). Agencies are the wedge, and they rarely want a vendor's brand on client deliverables.
- **The entry tier cannot finish the loop.** Solo's governed actions are "Draft only" (§8), so a solo owner can never complete an approved send, which is D's own moment in §5.4.
- **The highest-value moments are deferred.** Front doors and whispers wait for phase 2 (§10).
- **Some claims overreach.** "The CMO opened it twice" (§5.5) needs email-open tracking that no reference provides, which is inconsistent with the product's "no claim without a receipt" honesty.
- **Invites coworker sprawl.** "Hire a team in a sentence" and 100 coworkers on the Business tier encourage a Floor of strangers, the same casting problem as A.

### B: Ninth Floor (*Walk into your company. It's already working.*): **6.5**

**Strengths**
- **It has the most emotionally resonant owner story.** The Night Shift lamps, Door Plates, raised hands and "Quiet night. Nothing needs you." (§8.1). "Emotional ownership" (§11.5) is real: owners think of their business as a place.
- **"Everything that happens has a place, and nothing is faked"** (§2).
  - The FloorProjector derives every presence frame from events.
  - The exit criterion requires every frame to carry a `cause.seq` (§13).
  - This is the anti-gimmick guarantee, and it builds trust.
- **Ten-second comprehension is an owner job** ("Tell me what's happening in ten seconds", §3), and it is tested: 5 of 6 owners answer "what needs you?" within 10 s (§13).
- **It makes context scoping intuitive: the drop target is the scope** (§8.7). Dropping on a desk scopes to that agent, dropping on a room wall scopes to the project, and dropping on the lead lets the lead route it.
- **It has the safest voice approvals** (§8.5, §9). Standing grants and money need an on-screen tap after a spoken read-back, and voice alone grants only "once" or "task".
- **It gets meeting pacing right** (§7). Meetings run fast when nobody listens and paced when someone does. Skip lines take the owner back to the live edge, catch-up is capped at 15 s, and each agent gets a 90 s speaking budget. As §2 puts it: "Meetings slow down to human pace for you. Work never does."
- **Owners can talk to anyone without losing control** (§2.3). A conversation at a desk that changes scope reaches the lead as a **plan diff**, which lets the owner talk to anyone without two people steering the same project.
- **It has the most thorough accessibility and fallbacks** (§10). These include the Directory view, a keyboard map, "Just tell me" mode, Quiet Floor and Text Floor.

**Weaknesses**
- **It shows work more than it delivers it.** The jobs-to-be-done are about seeing and checking in (§3). There is no outcomes section, no time-saved report and no pricing. The "first demo" (§13) ends with the owner hearing an agent use her whisper, not with a delivered result. Owners pay for outcomes, not for watching.
- **The name is weak.** "Ninth Floor" nods to Cloud Nine (§1), which means nothing to a buyer. It says nothing about staff or AI, and could be read as a coworking space or a bar.
- **Several delights may not earn their cost.** Room tone, spatial murmur at −18 dB (§5.4) and the 90-second Rewind time-lapse (§8.8) are charming but have no clear value to a busy owner. Room tone is sensibly deferred; Rewind needs a job to justify it.
- **There is no path for trust to grow.** The Knock Budget and grants exist, but nothing moves an owner from approving everything toward delegating more.
- **It makes the Floor the home screen**, and §12 concedes that "metaphor UIs often lose to lists for repeated tasks".
- **Heavy jargon.** The vocabulary table (§4) has 16 terms (Pass, Tape, Door Plate, Mailroom, Knock Budget and more), so a new owner must learn a language before feeling any value.

### C: Mainspring (*One voice to direct it. One ledger to prove it.*): **6.0**

**Strengths**
- **It understands the owner's fear best** (§2). The owner has been "burned by automation that 'said it did it'", and "will delegate only if they can check the work, cap the spend and stop everything instantly." That is the most precise buyer insight in the set.
- **"Why did we…?"** (§6.7) is a killer feature for owners: institutional memory with evidence. The concept's example: "Why don't we deliver to small parties?" returns the meeting clip, the loss per delivery and the owner's own decision, replayable with faces.
- **The evidence contract** (§4.3) gives every acceptance criterion a declared proof: citation, receipt, artifact, metric with `asOf`, or owner answer. Trust comes from mechanism, not tone.
- **The exactly-once email survives a crash** (§6.5), ending in "42 of 42 sent, no duplicates". The owner never sees the machinery, but they would feel the absence of a double send.
- **It is honest about limits.**
  - "Waiting for the office computer, which is asleep" (§6.6), and work never moves silently to a paid cloud model.
  - `asOf` on business facts: "a price can be correct and still stale" (§4.8).
- **Every route has a sentence the owner hears** (§4.2), such as "Pricing huddle, ~6 min. Listen?"

**Weaknesses**
- **It is an engine specification, not a product.** The owner shows up in §5 as a table of surfaces. The signature experiences are three-sentence scenes followed by longer "Under the hood" lists.
- **The name and tagline are for engineers.** "Mainspring" is an elegant clockwork metaphor that is obscure to owners, and "ledger" in the tagline reads as accounting or crypto. Neither evokes staff or coworkers.
- **There is no ROI, pricing or onboarding story.** The MVP keeps metering but drops Stripe billing (§10).
- **The MVP is the least compelling to a buyer** (§10). It leaves out whispers, doors, billing and the spatial Floor, and presence is a "Floor-lite" grid of glyphs. Its exit criteria are engineering criteria.
- **Enterprise creep.** Azure, Bedrock and Vertex (§4.6) are irrelevant to a 3–40 person business.

## 4. Recommended name and positioning for the final product

- **Product name: *Headcount*** (D), category *The AI Office*, tagline *"Hire a team in a sentence. Watch it work. Walk in anytime."* Run a trademark and search-availability check before committing, because "headcount" is a common HR and finance term.
- **Lead agent role: "Chief of Staff".** The owner names it (A's "Righthand" can live on as marketing copy: "your right hand").
- **Product principles: A's three design laws, verbatim**, plus D's thesis "presence is observability for non-engineers" and B's "nothing is faked".
- **Owner vocabulary: at most about eight nouns.**
  - The nouns: Chief of Staff, Coworker, Project, Floor, Room, Raised hand / Inbox, Brief, Front door.
  - Do not ship "Engagement", "Pass", "Tape", "Mailroom" or "Knock Budget" as primary UI terms.

## 5. Ideas to graft into the final product (from any concept)

1. **The owner's rhythm, trimmed** (A §4). Default to three touchpoints: a spoken Morning Brief of 5 minutes or less, one Decision Deck sitting, and a weekly Review. The Brief is ranked by needs-the-owner, deadline and money at stake, and each item is phrased verb-first [BB `goalBoard`]. Make the Evening Wrap and Month-end Board optional.
2. **An autonomy dial per action class** (A §7.1). Ask, Propose, Act & report and Act quietly, compiled to BB grant kinds and scopes. Identity and access are always Ask.
3. **Track records with promotion and automatic demotion** (A §7.2, §5.7), with a résumé on the desk card ("22 of 24 posts as-is").
4. **A Time-back report merged with Headcount maths** (A §10 + D §7.5). Estimates are human-equivalent per plan step, set at approval and calibrated by the owner. The report shows hours saved, dollars spent, dollars per hour saved and decision latency, and it is the renewal artifact.
5. **Predictable money** (A §7.3 + D §8). Show a forecast range on every plan approval. Reserve budget before each call. An overrun raises a hand. The owner chooses per project whether hitting the cap stops work or raises a hand. Voice is paid for only when someone listens.
6. **The urgency ladder and a spoken handoff card** (A §5.4). Digest → Deck → push → live, gated by the BB annoyance budget and judge. The 15-second card covers who, what, why now, the recommendation and the options.
7. **Voice rules** (A §9). Audio plays only when asked for. A summary comes before any stream. Every Brief and replay has speed controls from 1× to 2×.
8. **Faces as the monitoring UI** (D §1) that **never fake work** (B §2). Every non-idle state carries an event cause. A quiet night is reported as a quiet night.
9. **The drop target is the scope** (B §8.7). A file dropped on a desk goes to that agent, on a room wall to the project, and on the Chief of Staff to be routed.
10. **Voice-approval safety** (B §8.5). Voice can grant only "once" or "task". Money and standing grants need an on-screen confirmation after a spoken read-back.
11. **Meeting pacing** (B §7). Meetings run fast with no listener and paced with one. Catch-up lasts at most 15–20 s, skip lines jump to the live edge, and each agent has a speaking budget.
12. **Desk conversations become plan diffs through the lead** (B §2.3), so the owner has one front door with every door open.
13. **"Just tell me" mode plus Directory parity** (B §10, BB Simple mode), tested for 10-second comprehension (B §13).
14. **Night Shift lamps** (B §8.1): overnight durable work, visible at a glance.
15. **Walkie-talkie mode with lock-screen controls and patch-in** (B §8.6; also A and D).
16. **"Why did we…?"** (C §6.7). A question about a past decision returns the decision, the verdict and the replayable meeting clip.
17. **An evidence contract per acceptance criterion** (C §4.3), plus an "unverified" badge on unsourced numbers (D §11).
18. **`asOf` on every business fact** (C §4.8), and **honest waiting reasons** instead of silent fallbacks (C §6.6).
19. **Seats for humans, not for coworkers, and one credit pool** (D §8), always displayed in dollars.
20. **The agency wedge and packs with an in-character sceptic** (D §9), plus a **keyless demo office** (D §9, BB Mock + PG seed).
21. **Front doors with real escalation** (D §5.6 + A §5.8). A customer asks for something only the owner can decide, it arrives in the Inbox, and the Chief of Staff proposes a follow-up project.
22. **The stand-up in your ears** (D §5.5). Director mode from real run state, interruptible, with decisions resolved by voice.
23. **A 60-minute first outcome in onboarding** (A §6). Three starters come from what the product just learned, connectors start read-only, and no model is called on empty input.
24. **A procedural bust holds the desk until the portrait paints** (D §5.1, OC), so a pod appears within seconds.
25. **A cross-family sceptic** (D §6): the sceptic runs on a different model family from the lead.

## 6. Must-fix problems for the final spec

1. **Lead with outcomes, not the office.** The north star should be *accepted deliverables* and *decisions resolved*. The counter-metric should be *owner minutes per accepted deliverable* (lower is better). Drop "walked into a room" as a success target (D §10), or keep it only as a diagnostic. Every demo ends on a delivered artifact (unlike B §13).
2. **No ritual overload.** Keep at most three default touchpoints, all rescheduleable or removable. The Brief has a hard 5-minute cap, "shorter" and "skip" feedback, and a tracked listen-through rate. A quiet day says "nothing needs you" (fixes A §4).
3. **Voice approvals of money or scale need a safety step.**
   - Voice alone can grant only once or task, and only under a per-workspace dollar or recipient cap.
   - Above the cap, and for any standing grant, require on-screen confirmation.
   - When the owner is driving, defer anything above the cap to the Deck rather than asking for a tap (fixes A §5.1).
4. **Reconcile growing autonomy with safety.** A lets send and publish rise to Act & report after promotion (§7.1). C says send, pay and publish **never** get standing grants (§4.10). The final rule:
   - Promotions become **bounded** standing grants: recipient class, amount cap, asset library and time window.
   - They are proposed from the track record, confirmed with a tap, and automatically demoted on any reversal.
   - Money is never Act quietly, and identity and access are always Ask.
5. **Staff from the existing team before hiring.**
   - The Chief of Staff reuses trained coworkers (keeping their track record and distilled memory) before casting new ones.
   - It shows "hire vs reuse" in the plan card and caps active coworkers per plan tier.
   - Without this rule, track records, the "trained staff" switching cost (D §7.2) and recognisable voices all fail.
6. **Isolate clients by default.** Memory is scoped per project and per client, with no cross-project recall unless the owner allows it. This answers the agency and practice fears named in A §2 (off-brand work, confidentiality) and A §12.
7. **Pricing must let the wedge finish the loop.**
   - The entry tier must allow at least one governed send with approval (D §8 Solo is "Draft only").
   - Client-facing platform branding must be removable on the tier agencies buy (D puts white-label at $799).
   - The AI-coworker disclosure badge [PG] stays on every tier.
8. **Show credits in dollars.** Every forecast, cap and report shows dollars first. Unknown cost is never shown as zero [BB ledger]. Per-model `rates` exist for every provider before any budget UI ships (the matrix §1.6 correction).
9. **Make time-saved claims credible.** Show ranges calibrated by the owner, derived from plan-step estimates, never a single inflated number. A forecast-versus-actual comparison appears in the weekly Review.
10. **No claims beyond the evidence.** Demos and the stand-up may state only what has a receipt, citation or metric behind it. D §5.5's "the CMO opened it twice" needs email-open tracking that no reference provides. Keep PG's "never claim a real-world action" rule, enforced by a receipt check.
11. **Avatars must stay optional.** The Floor is never the only way in. List and Directory parity, "Just tell me" mode, still-portrait mode and audio-only walkie mode all reach the same actions. The cost of many faces is bounded by LOD, and the 12-face spike runs first.
12. **Keep the vocabulary small and plain.** Ship about eight owner-facing nouns (see §4). Jargon from B §4 and A §3 must not reach first-run UI.
13. **Keep the MVP to one pack and one loop.**
    - One pack (Agency), one governed action (email send, exactly once), and one room at a time for listening in.
    - Spike first: live voice-front/brain-back, RoomDO fan-out, 12 faces plus one stage, and a ProjectDO that survives sleep with exactly-once effects.
    - No Azure, Bedrock or Vertex, and no marketplace, in the MVP.
14. **Onboarding must deliver value before connecting tools.** The first outcome must be reachable from pasted text, files and a URL alone. OAuth write scopes are asked for only at first need, as a Deck card (A §6).
15. **Make listening in fast and skippable.**
    - The first voiced line plays within about 2.5 s.
    - Catch-up lasts at most 20 s, and skip-to-live is always available.
    - Barge-in behaviour is defined: hold-to-talk cuts the speaker within 300 ms; "raise hand" yields at the end of the sentence. A §5.3 and D §5.3 currently disagree.
16. **Recording and whisper transparency.** Get recording consent before the owner's voice is stored in replays (C §6.7). Whispers stay on the owner's tape with a `quietly` or `attribute` policy (B §7) and are never shown to guests.
